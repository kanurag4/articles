  • In both cases, more than 70% of the valuation is terminal value — the 2.5% terminal growth assumption matters more than the 5-year forecast itself.

Both are still sitting on the live screens

Best Buy first appeared on the S&P 500 "Most Undervalued" screen on 26 June 2026, priced at $76.89. Today it's $85.25 — up 10.9% over 39 days. Sonic Healthcare first appeared on the ASX 300 screen on 19 June 2026, priced at A$20.05. Today it's A$22.09 — up 10.2% over 46 days.

Two stocks, two price moves in the direction each DCF flagged, over five to six weeks. That is not evidence the model works. It's two data points, in a period when broader equity markets were also generally rising. The tool's own output reports expected annualised return over 1, 3, and 5 years, never over six weeks — so a short-term move toward the flagged value isn't something the model even claims to predict, and it says nothing about whether the same call on the next flagged stock plays out the same way. Nothing that follows is a signal to act on either stock, now or going forward.

Both companies use standard DCF (neither is a bank or insurer). The discount rate — WACC — is built the same way for both: cost of equity via CAPM, blended with an estimated cost of debt, weighted by each company's own capital structure.

The two WACCs land on opposite sides of their sector reference ranges, for opposite mechanical reasons. Best Buy's 10.45% sits above its 7.5–8.5% retail range — its beta of 1.32 pushes cost of equity to 11.99%, and a light 19% debt weighting doesn't pull the blend down much. Sonic Healthcare's 6.72% sits below its 8.5–10.0% healthcare range — its beta of 0.59 keeps cost of equity to 8.33%, and a heavier 35% debt weighting (debt is cheaper than equity here) pulls the blended rate down further still.

The scenarios and the output

Both run a 5-year projection with a 2.5% terminal growth rate in every scenario. Growth and WACC move together across Bear/Base/Bull — lower growth pairs with a higher discount rate, higher growth with a lower one.

Where most of the number comes from

For both stocks, the tool flags terminal value over 70% of the total intrinsic value. That means the majority of both figures rests on a single assumption — cash flows growing at 2.5% forever after year five — not on the five-year forecast itself. A 2.5% terminal rate is in line with long-run economic growth, which is the standard sanity check for that input; the tool would flag a value of 4–5% as implying the company outgrows the entire economy indefinitely. Both scenarios pass that check, but it's worth naming explicitly, since it means the 5-year growth assumptions in the table above — however different they look between Bear, Base, and Bull — are doing less work than the fixed 2.5% figure repeated in every row.

Frequently asked questions

Is this article a recommendation to buy Best Buy or Sonic Healthcare?

No. Every figure here is mechanical output from running each company's cash flows through a Discounted Cash Flow formula — it is not a view on either company's news, management, or competitive position, and it is not a signal to act on either stock.

Why were Best Buy and Sonic Healthcare picked for this article?

Both currently sit on KashVector's live "Most Undervalued" screens, and both have been on those screens for over five weeks — long enough to show what happened to price since they were first flagged, using real, checkable dates rather than a hand-picked snapshot.

Does a stock's price moving toward the DCF's call prove the model works?

No. Two stocks moving in the flagged direction over five to six weeks is not evidence of anything — it is two data points, in a period when broader equity markets were also generally rising, and it says nothing about whether the same call on the next flagged stock, or on either of these stocks over a longer horizon, plays out the same way.

What does "terminal value over 70%" mean for these two stocks?

It means more than 70% of each stock's calculated intrinsic value comes from the perpetuity assumption beyond the five-year forecast, not from the forecast itself. That makes the terminal growth rate — 2.5% for both, in line with long-run economic growth — the single most sensitive input in both valuations.
